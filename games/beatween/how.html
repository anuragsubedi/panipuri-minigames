How Beatween works

Beatween plays one pure tone in your left ear and a slightly different one in your right. Your brain hears the gap between them as a slow pulse: a binaural beat.

Open Beatween

Two tones, one beat

If the left ear gets 200 Hz and the right gets 210 Hz, nothing in the air actually pulses. Each ear hears a steady tone. The pulse at 10 Hz (the difference) is put together inside your head, which is why it only works on headphones.

The sound

  • Two sine oscillators, the purest possible tone, routed hard left and hard right so neither ear hears the other.
  • Each ear can go from 40 to 1200 Hz. Binaural beats are clearest when both tones are below about 1000 Hz.
  • The beat goes up to 50 Hz, in steps of 0.5 Hz. Link keeps the beat fixed while you move either ear.
  • Volume is capped below full scale, and play fades in and out so there are no clicks.

The scope picture

The screen is an XY scope: the left tone moves a dot side to side, the right tone moves it up and down. Equal tones trace a tilted ellipse; tones an octave apart trace a figure-eight. As the beat runs, the shape slowly breathes.

x = sin(θ),   y = sin(ratio · θ + phase)

The picture deliberately does not flicker at the beat rate. Shapes changing 2–40 times a second can trigger photosensitive reactions, so the morph is slowed and capped at 0.9 changes per second.

Background noise

Optional noise plays the same in both ears, under the tones:

  • Pink: equal energy per octave (−3 dB per octave), like steady rain. Made by filtering random numbers.
  • Brown: deeper (−6 dB per octave), like a distant waterfall. Made by adding up random steps.

A note on claims

Research on binaural beats is mixed. Beatween is a pleasant tone tool, not medical advice. Keep the volume low.
