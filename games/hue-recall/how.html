How Hue Recall works

You see a color for a moment, then rebuild it with three sliders. The score measures how different your color looks to a human eye, not just how far apart the numbers are.

Play Hue Recall

A round

  1. A color fills the card for 5 seconds on Easy or 2 on Hard.
  2. It disappears, and you rebuild it with hue, saturation and brightness sliders.
  3. You see both colors side by side and your points. A game is 5 rounds.

Which colors come up

Targets are random, but saturation and brightness never go below 25%. Very dark or very grey colors all look alike, which would make rounds unfair. The hue slider starts at a random spot each round so you can't anchor to the same place.

How "close" is measured

Two colors can be far apart in slider numbers and still look nearly identical (or the other way round). So both colors are converted to CIELAB, a color space built to match human vision, and compared with the CIEDE2000 formula, the industry standard for "how different do these look?".

The result is called ΔE (delta E). Around 1 is the smallest difference most people can see; above 10 the colors look clearly different. The formula is checked against the published reference data in the tests.

From ΔE to points

points = 10 × e−(ΔE / 22)1.6
